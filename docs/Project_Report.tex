\chapter{INTRODUCTION}
\section{Introduction}
SellWise is an enterprise-grade Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) web application designed to help small and medium-sized retail businesses streamline their operational workflow. It consolidates point-of-sale (POS) operations, multi-tenant inventory management, customer behavioral analytics, salesperson attribution, printable invoicing, and machine learning-driven demand forecasting into a unified ecosystem. 

Even today, a large percentage of independent retail outlets rely on paper logs or rudimentary spreadsheets for ledger maintenance. These conventional approaches are error-prone, labor-intensive, lack data security, and offer zero forward-looking analytical visibility. SellWise bridges this technological gap by providing an intuitive digital infrastructure that automates transaction logging, eliminates data loss, and delivers proactive predictive insights.

To optimize scalability and maintain high responsiveness, the platform adopts a decoupled microservice architecture:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Core Web Layer:} Built with ASP.NET Core 10 MVC and Entity Framework Core 10, executing ACID-compliant business transactions, user authentication, multi-tenant session scoping, role-based authorization, and PDF document generation.
    \item \textbf{Predictive Analytics Engine:} A standalone asynchronous microservice implemented in Python with FastAPI, running Meta Prophet time-series regression models to compute 30-day demand projections with upper and lower uncertainty bounds.
\end{itemize}

\section{Project Overview}
SellWise provides an affordable, turnkey retail management suite requiring zero complex server configuration or specialized technical expertise from shop owners. 

The application utilizes database-level multi-tenancy where each registered business operates within an isolated workspace. Store data (products, orders, customers, and financial audits) is strictly segmented by unique Store Identifiers, preventing cross-tenant data leakage. By delegating data processing, RFM customer clustering, and mathematical forecasting to the software, business owners and store managers can focus on customer satisfaction and growth.

\section{Problem Statement}
Manual retail management imposes severe constraints on operational efficiency:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Inventory Imbalance \& Capital Lockup:} Overstocking slow-moving products freezes working capital, while stockouts of high-velocity items cause missed revenue opportunities and customer churn.
    \item \textbf{Customer Attrition:} Without automated purchase history analysis, businesses fail to recognize when loyal buyers become inactive.
    \item \textbf{Decentralized Financial Records:} Logging sales, supplier expenses, and operational overhead across disparate books obscures actual net profitability.
    \item \textbf{Absence of Predictive Planning:} Historical reports only describe past events without projecting future inventory demand or seasonal spikes.
\end{itemize}

\section{Motivation}
Our motivation in developing SellWise stems from the desire to democratize advanced enterprise analytics for small businesses:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Empower retail owners with a single real-time executive dashboard summarizing revenue, orders, and restock urgencies.
    \item Equip stores with AI-based 30-day demand forecasts to optimize reorder cycles and inventory capital.
    \item Automate customer retention using Recency, Frequency, and Monetary (RFM) segmentation.
    \item Ensure atomic transaction integrity during high-throughput checkouts.
    \item Provide built-in printable invoices and automated PDF financial audit reports.
\end{itemize}

\section{Proposed System}
The proposed system implements a three-tier Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) hierarchy:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Business Owner:} Full administrative privileges, store provisioning, multi-store switching, employee management, financial analytics, expense tracking, and PDF audit exports.
    \item \textbf{Store Manager:} Catalog management, stock updates, order processing, customer directory management, and expense logging.
    \item \textbf{Salesman / Employee:} High-speed POS checkout, customer lookup, customer registration, and product catalog lookup (restricted from sensitive financial summaries and staff administration).
\end{itemize}

When an order is submitted, an EF Core transaction validates available stock, deducts inventory, assigns customer order history, and records the active salesperson. In parallel, background analytics query historical sales trends to update demand forecasts.

\section{Purpose Of The System}
The purpose of SellWise is to provide a comprehensive, all-in-one digital operating system for small retailers. By unifying POS checkouts, inventory health checks, customer segmentation, and predictive forecasting, retailers eliminate software fragmentation and make informed, data-driven decisions.

\section{Goal}
The primary goal is to deliver an accessible, high-performance web platform that automates routine retail bookkeeping, prevents stockouts through predictive modeling, and enhances business profitability.

\subsection{Transactional POS Order Creation with Salesperson Tracking}
The order processing pipeline uses database transactions to guarantee that product stock deduction, customer total accumulation, order channel classification (\textit{Walk-In, Online, Delivery, Phone}), and salesperson attribution are committed atomically:

\begin{lstlisting}[language={[Sharp]C}]
public async Task<string?> CreateOrderAsync(Guid storeId, OrderFormViewModel model, string? salespersonName)
{
    using var transaction = await _db.Database.BeginTransactionAsync();
    try
    {
        var order = new Order
        {
            StoreId = storeId,
            CustomerId = model.CustomerId,
            OrderNumber = $"ORD-{DateTime.UtcNow:yyyyMMdd}-{Guid.NewGuid().ToString()[..6]}",
            OrderType = model.OrderType ?? "Walk-In",
            SalespersonName = salespersonName,
            Status = "pending",
            DeliveryCharge = model.DeliveryCharge,
            Discount = model.Discount,
            Notes = model.Notes,
            OrderDate = DateTime.UtcNow
        };

        decimal subtotal = 0;
        foreach (var item in model.Items.Where(i => i.ProductId != Guid.Empty && i.Quantity > 0))
        {
            var product = await _db.Products.FirstOrDefaultAsync(p => p.Id == item.ProductId && p.StoreId == storeId);
            if (product == null || product.StockQuantity < item.Quantity)
            {
                await transaction.RollbackAsync();
                return $"Insufficient stock for {product?.Name ?? "item"}.";
            }

            product.StockQuantity -= item.Quantity;
            var lineTotal = item.Quantity * product.SellingPrice;
            subtotal += lineTotal;

            order.Items.Add(new OrderItem
            {
                ProductId = product.Id,
                ProductName = product.Name,
                Quantity = item.Quantity,
                UnitPrice = product.SellingPrice
            });
        }

        order.Total = Math.Max(0, subtotal + order.DeliveryCharge - order.Discount);
        _db.Orders.Add(order);
        await _db.SaveChangesAsync();
        await transaction.CommitAsync();
        return null;
    }
    catch (Exception ex)
    {
        await transaction.RollbackAsync();
        return "Transaction failed during order checkout.";
    }
}
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Proactive Inventory Alerts \& Automated Resolution}
The alert service runs periodic scans across store inventory. If stock falls below the configured threshold, critical or warning alerts are generated. When stock is replenished, the alerts automatically transition to resolved:

\begin{lstlisting}[language={[Sharp]C}]
public async Task ScanAndGenerateAlertsAsync(Guid storeId)
{
    var healthyProductIds = await _db.Products
        .Where(p => p.StoreId == storeId && p.IsActive && p.StockQuantity > p.LowStockThreshold)
        .Select(p => p.Id).ToListAsync();

    var alertsToResolve = await _db.Alerts
        .Where(a => a.StoreId == storeId && a.Type == "Low Stock" && healthyProductIds.Contains(a.ProductId))
        .ToListAsync();

    foreach (var alert in alertsToResolve)
    {
        alert.Type = "Low Stock (Resolved)";
        alert.IsRead = true;
    }

    var lowStockProducts = await _db.Products
        .Where(p => p.StoreId == storeId && p.IsActive && p.StockQuantity <= p.LowStockThreshold)
        .ToListAsync();

    var existingAlertProductIds = await _db.Alerts
        .Where(a => a.StoreId == storeId && a.Type == "Low Stock")
        .Select(a => a.ProductId).ToListAsync();

    foreach (var product in lowStockProducts)
    {
        if (existingAlertProductIds.Contains(product.Id)) continue;

        _db.Alerts.Add(new InventoryAlert
        {
            StoreId = storeId,
            ProductId = product.Id,
            Type = "Low Stock",
            Severity = product.StockQuantity == 0 ? "Critical" : "Warning",
            Message = product.StockQuantity == 0
                ? $"Out of stock. Threshold is {product.LowStockThreshold}."
                : $"Only {product.StockQuantity} unit(s) left (Threshold: {product.LowStockThreshold}).",
            IsRead = false,
            CreatedAt = DateTime.UtcNow
        });
    }
    await _db.SaveChangesAsync();
}
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Machine Learning Demand Forecasting Service}
The Python FastAPI microservice processes historical daily order volumes using Meta Prophet, outputting 30-day point estimates and confidence intervals:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=python]
from prophet import Prophet
import pandas as pd

def generate_forecast(history, periods=30):
    if len(history) < 2:
        return []

    df = pd.DataFrame([{"ds": point.ds, "y": point.y} for point in history])
    df = df.groupby("ds", as_index=False)["y"].sum().sort_values("ds").reset_index(drop=True)

    span_days = (df["ds"].max() - df["ds"].min()).days
    has_yearly_signal = span_days >= 365

    model = Prophet(
        yearly_seasonality=has_yearly_signal,
        weekly_seasonality=True,
        daily_seasonality=False
    )
    try:
        model.fit(df)
    except Exception as e:
        return []

    future = model.make_future_dataframe(periods=periods)
    forecast_df = model.predict(future)
    future_forecast = forecast_df.tail(periods)

    return [
        {
            "ds": row['ds'].date(),
            "yhat": max(0, float(row['yhat'])),
            "yhat_lower": max(0, float(row['yhat_lower'])),
            "yhat_upper": max(0, float(row['yhat_upper']))
        }
        for _, row in future_forecast.iterrows()
    ]
\end{lstlisting}

Launch the environment using the automated startup script:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=bash]
# macOS / Linux
./start.sh

# Windows PowerShell
.\start.ps1
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Accessing the System}
Navigate to \url{http://localhost:5000}.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Default Administrator:} \texttt{admin@sellwise.com} / \texttt{Admin123!}
    \item \textbf{Pre-configured Demo Stores:} \textit{SellWise Tech BD} (Electronics), \textit{StyleHub BD} (Fashion).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Processing a Point-of-Sale (POS) Order}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Select \textbf{Orders $\rightarrow$ New Order} from the navigation bar.
    \item Click products from the catalog to add them to the active cart.
    \item Select or create a customer using the Quick-Create modal.
    \item Choose the order type (\textit{Walk-In, Online, Delivery, Phone}) and enter any discount or delivery fee.
    \item Click \textbf{Complete Order} to finalize the transaction.
    \item Click \textbf{Print Invoice} to generate a formatted printable thermal or standard receipt.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Reviewing Demand Forecasts}
On the \textbf{Dashboard}, inspect the 30-day forecast cards. Products flagged with high projected demand and low current stock indicate an immediate need to reorder.

\chapter{CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORKS}

\section{Conclusion}
SellWise successfully delivers a robust, accessible, and AI-enabled retail management platform. By uniting transactional POS operations, multi-tenant inventory control, RFM customer segmentation, salesperson tracking, printable invoicing, and machine learning demand forecasting, SellWise empowers small business owners to operate with enterprise-level efficiency.

\section{Key Outcomes}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Replaced manual, error-prone paper registers with an automated ACID-compliant web application.
    \item Integrated Meta Prophet time-series models to provide reliable 30-day demand forecasts.
    \item Implemented dynamic customer grouping based on RFM scores to guide targeted retention.
    \item Guaranteed zero cross-tenant data leakage via session-scoped store query filters.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future Enhancements}

    \item \textbf{Multi-Warehouse Routing:} Managing shared inventory across distributed warehouse locations with inter-branch transfer tracking.
\end{itemize}
